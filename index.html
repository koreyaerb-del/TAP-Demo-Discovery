5The five Ava questions, and what “Expert” sounds like

From the rubric Ava generated for this JD. Use these to talk Marcus and Dana through the scores.

Q1Situational

A conveyor sensor keeps tripping the alarm, but the belt is clear. You've checked twice and the supervisor is frustrated. What's your next move?

Expert (2)Tests the sensor (continuity, response, alignment), checks wiring for loose or corroded connections, narrows the problem down step by step, and explains it to the supervisor.
Needs Practice (0)Swaps the sensor without testing it, or says "I don't know."
Q2Behavioral

Describe a time you used a manual or schematic on equipment you'd never worked on.

Expert (2)Found the right document, read it systematically, asked an experienced tech, and applied what they learned again later.
Needs Practice (0)Guessed, skipped the manual, or gave up.
Q3Technical

Walk me through safely measuring AC voltage across a relay coil in a 120V control circuit.

Expert (2)Sets the meter to AC volts on a 200V+ range, puts the probes on the coil terminals without shorting anything else, reads the voltage, and explains what the reading means.
Needs Practice (0)Sets the meter to DC, picks the wrong range, or bridges terminals.
Q4Knowledge

Why is arc flash a hazard, and what does NFPA 70E tell you to do?

Expert (2)Explains arc flash as an explosion of heat and light that causes burns. Cites 70E: a risk assessment, PPE, approach boundaries and LOTO.
Needs Practice (0)Doesn't know what it is, or treats it as minor.
Q5Behavioral

Tell me about a time you found an equipment problem that could have caused a safety issue or a repeat failure.

Expert (2)A concrete hazard. Reported it right away, secured the equipment, found the root cause, and documented a preventive step.
Needs Practice (0)Ignored it, hid it, or blamed someone else.

Closing line after Q5: “Five questions, about five minutes, on a phone. Situational, behavioral, technical and safety, all built from your job description and scored the same way for every candidate. Your recruiters start here instead of a 10-minute phone call.”

6Job description to paste into Ava

Hit Copy, then paste it into Ava. Say: “This is the kind of JD you already have. Nothing special.”

Industrial Maintenance Technician – Electrical
Rio Grande Fabrication (RGF) · San Antonio, TX · Full-time, 2nd shift

About the role
Keep our fabrication lines running safely. You'll troubleshoot and repair electrical and mechanical problems on conveyors, motors, presses, welding cells and control panels, and help prevent repeat breakdowns.

What you'll do
- Respond to equipment breakdowns and find the root cause quickly and safely
- Troubleshoot 480V 3-phase power and 120V/24V control circuits, motors, relays, sensors and photo-eyes
- Read electrical schematics, ladder diagrams and equipment manuals
- Reset VFDs and basic PLC-controlled equipment
- Fix conveyor jams, belt tracking, bearings and gearboxes
- Perform preventive maintenance and log work in our maintenance system
- Follow lockout/tagout (LOTO) and NFPA 70E arc-flash safety on every job

What you need
- 2+ years of industrial maintenance experience, OR a trade school, military or community college electrical/maintenance program
- Comfortable using a multimeter and clamp meter
- Able to read schematics
- Working knowledge of LOTO and electrical safety
- Able to work 2nd shift, rotating Saturdays and overtime

Nice to have
- PLC experience (Allen-Bradley)
- Bilingual English/Spanish

Success in 90 days
- Fix common conveyor, motor and control-circuit faults on your own
- Complete PMs on time
- Zero safety incidents
